Hi, I'm Buse

Pronounced (boo-SEH)

PhD Student · Virginia Tech · SAIL Lab

I'm a 4th year PhD student in Computer Science at Virginia Tech, working with Dr. Eugenia Rho in the SAIL Lab. My research sits at the intersection of HCI, NLP, and computational social science.

I study how AI shapes the way people communicate, both with one another and with AI systems. My work spans interpersonal communication, emotional support, and collaboration, and much of it centers neurodivergent communities. I combine qualitative and participatory methods with computational analysis, including LLM auditing and multi-agent simulation, to understand people’s experiences with AI, the assumptions these systems make about their users, and the social dynamics that emerge around them.

I'll be on the 2027–2028 academic job market. I am seeking postdoctoral positions. If you know of a position that might be a good fit, I'd love to hear from you!

Research

Conversational Dynamics Between Autistic and Neurotypical IndividualsOngoing

We examine how autistic and neurotypical adults communicate and experience conversations with partners of the same or different neurotypes. Through real-time text-based conversations across autistic–autistic, autistic–neurotypical, and neurotypical–neurotypical pairs, we investigate mutual understanding, rapport, and conversational difficulties. Participants also reflect on specific moments in their conversations, allowing us to explore how people interpret and experience the same interaction.

Interpersonal CommunicationComputer-Mediated CommunicationNeurodiversitySocial Interaction

Emergent Leadership in LLM Multi-Agent SystemsOngoing

We investigate how leadership emerges when LLM agents collaborate on group decision-making tasks without predefined leaders. Using hidden-profile tasks in which agents possess different pieces of information, we examine how they share and integrate information, influence one another, and reach collective decisions. Our goal is to understand the conversational and collaborative dynamics through which agents take on leadership roles.

LLM Multi-Agent SystemsEmergent LeadershipHuman–AI CollaborationGroup Decision-Making

Designing Multimodal AI Support for Negative Self-TalkUnder review

We conducted four participatory focus groups with 12 autistic adults to explore how AI could support people experiencing negative self-talk. Participants engaged with visual, auditory, tactile, and embodied technologies, including a social robot and vibrotactile devices, and developed their own ideas for AI support. We examined how they envisioned combining different modalities, maintaining control over AI interactions, and incorporating support into everyday situations.

Human–AI InteractionParticipatory DesignMultimodal InteractionAutismMental Health

Autistic Adults' Perspectives on LLM Support for Negative Self-TalkCHI 2025

We surveyed autistic adults to understand how they envision using LLMs to manage negative self-talk and what shapes their willingness to seek AI-based support. We examined preferred forms of support, challenges in communicating their needs through prompts, and concerns about privacy, safety, and neurotypical assumptions embedded in LLM responses. Our findings highlight opportunities and barriers for making AI-based emotional support more responsive to autistic individuals' needs.

LLM Use in Neurodivergent CommunitiesGROUP 2025

We analyzed more than 55,000 Reddit posts and comments to understand how neurodivergent communities use and experience LLMs in everyday life. We identified uses ranging from emotional support to interpreting social situations and navigating interpersonal communication, alongside challenges involving neurotypical assumptions and responses that do not reflect users' intended voices. We also examined the workarounds community members develop to adapt these systems to their needs.

LLMsNeurodiversityOnline CommunitiesHuman–AI InteractionSocial Computing

Why People Do or Do Not Respond to Hate Speech OnlineTOCHI 2025

We surveyed hundreds of participants to investigate what motivates people to respond to hate speech online and what prevents them from speaking up. We found that individuals targeted by hate speech were more likely to respond, while younger individuals, women, and frequent witnesses reported greater hesitation, particularly due to fears of retaliation. These findings reveal how personal experiences and perceived risks shape responses to online hate.

Accessibility statement

I want this site to work for everyone, including people who use screen readers, keyboards, voice control, or magnification, and people who need different colors, fonts, or spacing. I aim to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, level AA.

What you can change

Open Accessibility in the top menu to find these settings. Your choices are saved in your browser only.

  • OpenDyslexic font
  • High contrast (also turned on automatically if your device asks for more contrast)
  • Text size: default, large, or larger
  • More space between lines, words, and letters
  • Narrower lines of text
  • A simplified, calmer layout without tinted backgrounds or hover effects
  • Reduced motion (on by default)
  • Light, dark, or system color theme

Other support

  • A "Skip to main content" link, full keyboard navigation, and visible focus outlines
  • Each page has its own web address, so the Back button and shared links work
  • Text descriptions for images and figures
  • Support for Windows High Contrast and other forced-colors modes

Known limitations

  • The CV in PDF form is not yet a tagged (screen-reader-ready) PDF. The same content is on the CV page as text.
  • Some paper links lead to publisher websites that I do not control.
  • I have checked this site with automated tools and by keyboard. I have not yet completed a full screen-reader review.
